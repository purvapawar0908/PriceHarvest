01 · About

What is PriceHarvest?

Agricultural crop prices fluctuate due to seasonal variations, supply-demand dynamics, and regional market factors. Understanding these patterns is critical for farmers, traders, and policymakers.

PriceHarvest analyzes and predicts crop prices using 5.5 years of historical mandi data from the Agmarknet Portal, Government of India (Jan 2021 – Jun 2026) for Onion, Potato and Cabbage across Mumbai, Nagpur and Nashik — combining EDA, time-series analysis, EDA dashboards,Charts and machine-learning prediction.

📊

Exploratory Analysis

Price distributions, outliers, statistical summaries, histograms, box plots and market comparison charts across Onion, Potato & Cabbage in Mumbai, Nagpur and Nashik.

📈

Trend Identification

Time-series decomposition into trend, seasonal and residual components. Rolling averages, lag features and calendar patterns over 5.5 years of daily prices.

🗺️

Regional Comparison

Side-by-side comparison of Mumbai, Nagpur and Nashik markets. Seasonal heatmaps and volatility views reveal city-level pricing differences for each crop.

💡

Price Intelligence

Narrative insights engine — seasonal peaks, volatility rankings, market anomalies and city-wise pricing gaps summarised clearly for decision support.

🤖

Price Prediction

XGBoost regression models trained separately for each of the nine City–Crop combinations using lag, rolling and calendar features.

02 · Key Insights

What the data reveals

01

Strong Seasonality

Onion and Potato show clear seasonal peaks and troughs. Calendar features (month, quarter, day-of-year) help the models capture these repeating patterns.

Seasonal cycles
02

City-level Price Gaps

The same crop can trade at noticeably different modal prices in Mumbai vs Nagpur vs Nashik on the same day, reflecting local supply, demand and logistics.

Mumbai · Nagpur · Nashik
03

Potato — Steady City Variation

Potato prices are less extreme than some other vegetables but still show consistent city-wise differences that the per-city XGBoost models are designed to capture.

Per-city models
04

5.5 Years of Post-COVID Data

Using Jan 2021 – Jun 2026 captures the post-COVID recovery period and multiple full seasonal cycles, giving models richer temporal context than shorter windows.

2021 → mid-2026
05

Interactive EDA Exploration

Python-based EDA performed in Google Colab was used to explore crop price trends, city-wise comparisons, seasonal patterns, and price movements through interactive visualizations.

Python EDA Analytics
06

District / City Intelligence

Dashboard and EDA views highlight clear pricing differences between Mumbai, Nagpur and Nashik markets for Onion, Potato and Cabbage.

City Comparison
07

Cabbage Price Behaviour

Cabbage prices show noticeable variation across Mumbai, Nagpur and Nashik, with occasional sharp price spikes. Nagpur has the highest average price, while Nashik has the lowest, indicating clear regional differences in cabbage price behaviour.

Price Variation

06 · Machine Learning

ML Insights & Prediction

Separate XGBoost regression models for each of the nine City–Crop pairs (Mumbai / Nagpur / Nashik × Onion / Potato / Cabbage). Train: Jan 2021 – Dec 2025 · Test: Jan 2026 – Jun 2026.

01

Nine City–Crop Models

Independent XGBoost models for Mumbai–Onion, Mumbai–Potato, Mumbai–Cabbage, Nashik–Onion, Nashik–Potato, Nashik–Cabbage, Nagpur–Onion, Nagpur–Potato, and Nagpur–Cabbage, using lag, rolling, and calendar features.

9 models · daily modal price
02

Feature Engineering

Calendar features, lag prices (1-30 days), rolling mean/std/min/max (7/14/30) and price-change features — all carefully shifted to avoid data leakage.

Lags · Rolling · Calendar
03

Evaluation Metrics

Models evaluated with MAE and MAPE on the hold-out 2026 period. Mumbai–Onion achieved the best MAPE (7.7%). Nashik–Onion is flagged as a high-error anomaly case.

MAE · MAPE
04

Actual vs Predicted

Residual checks and actual-vs-predicted plots show that models capture overall level and seasonality well; short-term spikes remain harder to track.

Residual analysis
05

Known Limitations

External drivers (weather, arrivals volume, policy) are not yet in the feature set. High-volatility days and the Nashik–Onion anomaly produce larger errors.

External factors pending
06

Next Steps

Improve outlier handling (especially Nashik–Onion), add weather & market features, expand to more crops, and expose live prediction on the website.

more crops · Live prediction
